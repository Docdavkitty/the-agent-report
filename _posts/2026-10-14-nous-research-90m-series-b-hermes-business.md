## TL;DR

- Nous Research closed a $90 million Series B at a $1.5 billion valuation, led by Robot Ventures, bringing total funding to $158 million.
- Its Hermes agent is free and MIT-licensed, has been cloned more than 24 million times, and by the company's own estimate drives about 2.5% of global AI token usage.
- The business case is not the code: it is "Hermes for Businesses," a governance and private-deployment layer sold back to companies that can already download the software for nothing.
- Nous reported roughly $36 million in annualized revenue in mid-September and expects to pass $100 million before the end of 2026.

## The uncomfortable pitch

Nous Research has spent three years giving its work away. The lab made its name fine-tuning Meta's Llama models and publishing open research, then released the Hermes agent under the permissive MIT license in February 2026. *(Source : [TechCrunch — Nous Research confirms it hit $1.5B Valuation](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/nous-research-confirms-it-hit-1-5b-valuation-launches-ai-agents-for-business-users/))*

That strategy produced extraordinary distribution. Hermes has been cloned more than 24 million times, and the Wall Street Journal counted over 22 million downloads. Nous's own internal estimate puts Hermes at roughly 2.5% of global AI token usage. *(Source : [TechFundingNews — Nous Research raises $90M](https://techfundingnews.com/nous-research-raises-90m-at-a-reported-1-5b-valuation-to-take-its-open-source-agent-to-business/))*

Now the company has to convert that into invoices. The $90 million round announced on October 7 — led by Robot Ventures, with Nvidia, Microsoft's M12, Samsung, Union Square Ventures, Menlo Ventures, Y Combinator, and 1789 Capital participating — is explicitly earmarked for "Hermes for Businesses." *(Source : [TechCrunch — Nous Research confirms it hit $1.5B Valuation](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/nous-research-confirms-it-hit-1-5b-valuation-launches-ai-agents-for-business-users/))*

The pitch to buyers is deliberately not about model quality. Nous says enterprises will be able to choose models by price and goal, keep their AI usage private, and own their institutional knowledge. Users can already run Hermes themselves or pay for hosted plans between $20 and $200 a month. The enterprise tier sells the things a free download cannot provide: governance, auditability, private deployment.

## What the numbers actually say

The revenue trajectory is the interesting part, because it sits on the boundary between a developer tool and a platform.

Nous was at roughly $36 million in annualized revenue by mid-September 2026 and expects to pass $100 million before the end of the year, according to the Wall Street Journal. At the reported $1.5 billion valuation, that is about 42 times the mid-September run rate — or roughly 15 times the optimistic year-end figure.

Nous itself has not confirmed the valuation; only $158 million in total funding is on the record, including a $50 million Series A led by Paradigm in April 2025. *(Source : [TechFundingNews — Nous Research raises $90M](https://techfundingnews.com/nous-research-raises-90m-at-a-reported-1-5b-valuation-to-take-its-open-source-agent-to-business/))*

Read that against the download count and the picture sharpens. Twenty-four million clones producing $36 million of annualized revenue is a conversion rate of well under a tenth of a cent per clone per year. The distribution is not the problem. Monetization is. The entire Series B is a bet that a few thousand enterprises will pay five- or six-figure sums for a layer that sits on top of software their engineers could otherwise pull for free.

## The competitive frame is open versus open

Hermes shipped weeks after OpenClaw, the open-source agent that TechCrunch treats as its direct rival. OpenAI hired OpenClaw's creator in February, and Nvidia — now a Nous investor — has reportedly built an enterprise platform on OpenClaw. *(Source : [TechFundingNews — Nous Research raises $90M](https://techfundingnews.com/nous-research-raises-90m-at-a-reported-1-5b-valuation-to-take-its-open-source-agent-to-business/))*

That is an unusually tangled competitive map. The same chip vendor backing Nous also runs a platform on its main open-source competitor. Meanwhile OpenAI and Anthropic sell closed agent products to the exact buyers Nous is now courting.

The market being contested is large enough to hold several winners. Grand View Research estimates AI agents were worth $7.6 billion in 2025 and will reach $182.9 billion by 2033, a 49.6% compound annual growth rate from 2026. Enterprise agentic AI specifically should grow from $5.3 billion in 2026 to $24.5 billion by 2030. *(Source : [TechFundingNews — Nous Research raises $90M](https://techfundingnews.com/nous-research-raises-90m-at-a-reported-1-5b-valuation-to-take-its-open-source-agent-to-business/))*

The sector is already crowded. Dust raised $40 million in a Series B led by Abstract and Sequoia in May for a workspace where people and agents share context. Bespoke Labs raised $40 million across seed and Series A to build simulated company environments where agents practice long tasks. Berlin-based telli raised $15 million in July for voice, WhatsApp, chat and email agents. Stuut raised a $52.5 million Series B on the same day as Nous's announcement for agents that chase unpaid invoices. *(Source : [TechFundingNews — Nous Research raises $90M](https://techfundingnews.com/nous-research-raises-90m-at-a-reported-1-5b-valuation-to-take-its-open-source-agent-to-business/))*

## The question buyers are actually asking

The governance layer Nous wants to sell is the layer enterprises are already anxious about. Palma.ai chief executive Patrick Eden argued this month that the single riskiest AI system in a company may be the agent that already holds its credentials — an agent that inherited access rather than being granted it. *(Source : [TechFundingNews — Nous Research raises $90M](https://techfundingnews.com/nous-research-raises-90m-at-a-reported-1-5b-valuation-to-take-its-open-source-agent-to-business/))*

That is a real market for Nous's proposition, but it is also the hardest thing to sell against free. Every enterprise buyer now has to answer the same comparison: pay a vendor for governance around an open agent, or take the code, run it in-house, and build the controls with the security team they already employ.

The $36 million run rate suggests some companies have already picked the first option. The next two quarters will show whether the $100 million target reflects genuine demand or a handful of large pilots.

## FAQ

**Is Hermes the same product as the Hermes models on Hugging Face?**
No. The Hermes name covers both a family of fine-tuned open-weight models that Nous began publishing in 2023, and the Hermes agent released under MIT in February 2026. The agent keeps memory across sessions, writes reusable skills as it works, searches the web, runs code, and handles scheduled tasks.

**Why is the license relevant to the valuation?**
Because MIT licensing makes the core product copyable by anyone, including competitors. Nous's only durable assets are the brand, the iteration speed, and the enterprise layer. Investors are pricing the layer, not the code.

**Who founded Nous Research?**
Jeffrey Quesnelle, Karan Malhotra, Ryan Teknium and Shivani Mitra founded the company in 2023. *(Source : [TechFundingNews — Nous Research raises $90M](https://techfundingnews.com/nous-research-raises-90m-at-a-reported-1-5b-valuation-to-take-its-open-source-agent-to-business/))*

**Is a founder-adjacent political investor unusual here?**
1789 Capital, where Donald Trump Jr. is a partner, took part in the round. It is one more signal that AI infrastructure funding in 2026 has become a political as well as a technical market.
