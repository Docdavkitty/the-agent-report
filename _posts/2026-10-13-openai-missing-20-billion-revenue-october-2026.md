## TL;DR

- OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue was approaching $50bn at the end of September 2026 — roughly $20bn below the $68–70bn figure that circulated in late September *(Source : [Financial Times — OpenAI annualised revenues $20bn less than previously signalled](https://www.ft.com/content/b66a9858-f8fb-46cb-b506-44bfe26fca2a))*.
- The gap is largely a measurement artefact, not lost sales: the higher number grossed up partner sales the way rival Anthropic does, while OpenAI reports its own net share *(Source : [CNBC — Nvidia, Oracle, CoreWeave and other AI stocks sink on OpenAI revenue report](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/08/open-ai-revenue-nvidia-oracle-coreweave.html))*.
- The correction triggered a selloff in the companies whose order books are written against OpenAI's projected spend — Nvidia fell about 3%, Oracle nearly 6% and CoreWeave nearly 8% *(Source : [CNN — Tech stocks drop after report that OpenAI's revenue is lower than expected](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/08/investing/openai-nasdaq-stocks))*.
- OpenAI reportedly still expects to reach or exceed $70bn in annualized revenue by end-2026, while its IPO is now off until at least 2027 *(Source : [Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance — OpenAI projects $70 billion annualized revenue by end of 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-projects-70-billion-annualized-113114847.html))*.

On 8 October 2026, the Financial Times reported that OpenAI had told investors its annualized revenue was approaching $50bn at the end of September — about $20bn below the roughly $68bn to $70bn figure that news outlets had carried only a week earlier. The reaction was immediate and public, even though the subject company is private: the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.4%, its worst day since July, as chipmakers and infrastructure names sold off *(Source : [CNN — Tech stocks drop after report that OpenAI's revenue is lower than expected](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/08/investing/openai-nasdaq-stocks))*.

Why does a footnote in a private company's investor deck move hundreds of billions in public market value? Because OpenAI is now the demand anchor of the AI capital cycle: its projected spending underwrites the order books of Nvidia, Oracle and CoreWeave. When the revenue meant to fund that spending is restated downward, the market re-prices the whole chain. The deeper story, though, is definitional — the $18bn–$20bn gap is as much an accounting artefact as a demand signal.

## What 'annualized revenue run rate' actually measures

A run rate is not revenue. It is an extrapolation: take a recent slice of sales — a month or a quarter — and multiply it out to a full year, as if current conditions persist *(Source : [Benzinga — OpenAI Revenue $20B Below Previous Reports, ORCL, NVDA Tumble](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/prediction-markets/26/10/62257221/openai-revenue-run-rate-ai-stocks))*. Bloomberg describes the $70bn figure as "a projection of OpenAI's yearly sales based on a shorter period" *(Source : [Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance — OpenAI projects $70 billion annualized revenue by end of 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-projects-70-billion-annualized-113114847.html))*. It is a pace, not a tally.

That distinction has three sharp consequences. First, a run rate is path-dependent: a company whose fastest months come late will show an exit pace well above its full-year total. The FT reported in September that OpenAI's own five-year deck projected $36bn of revenue for full-year 2026, rising to $350bn by 2030, alongside negative free cash flow of $278bn through 2030 *(Source : [Reuters — OpenAI forecasts cash burn near $280 billion by 2030, FT reports](https://www.reuters.com/technology/openai-expects-burn-through-almost-280-billion-by-2030-ft-reports-2026-09-18/))*. A ~$50bn exit rate can sit comfortably above a $36bn full-year line — they are simply different counts, and neither is dishonest.

Second, a run rate flatters lumpy businesses: enterprise contracts are often annual or multi-year and recognised unevenly, so annualizing a month in which several large deals landed — then applying a growth rate to that elevated base — compounds the distortion. Third, a run rate is unaudited: none of these figures is an OpenAI disclosure, but each was inferred by investors and relayed by people familiar with the matter *(Source : [CNBC — Nvidia, Oracle, CoreWeave and other AI stocks sink on OpenAI revenue report](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/08/open-ai-revenue-nvidia-oracle-coreweave.html))*.

## Why the gap is an accounting artefact, not a demand collapse

The $18bn–$20bn gap traces to a comparability exercise. Investors wanted to measure OpenAI against Anthropic, whose annualized revenue reached $65bn at the end of July *(Source : [CNBC — Anthropic says annualized revenue climbed to $65 billion in July](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/17/anthropic-says-annualized-revenue-climbed-to-65-billion-in-july.html))*. Anthropic counts the gross value of sales made through its cloud partners, such as AWS and Google Cloud; OpenAI counts only its own net share and leaves partner sales out *(Source : [TechCrunch — OpenAI's revenue is reportedly $20 billion less than previously projected](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/openais-revenue-is-reportedly-20-billion-less-than-previously-projected/))*. To compare like with like, OpenAI's numbers were grossed up to match Anthropic's method, then a growth rate applied on top — consistent with the 77% total and 107% enterprise run-rate growth OpenAI reported for the third quarter *(Source : [CNBC — Nvidia, Oracle, CoreWeave and other AI stocks sink on OpenAI revenue report](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/08/open-ai-revenue-nvidia-oracle-coreweave.html))*. One source told CNN the $70bn figure did not come from OpenAI at all *(Source : [CNN — Tech stocks drop after report that OpenAI's revenue is lower than expected](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/08/investing/openai-nasdaq-stocks))*. The $20bn "hole" is thus the distance between two definitions stacked on top of each other, not a shortfall in orders.

## The knock-on effect: compute is booked against the projection

The market selloff is the part that is real, and it is rational in structure even if triggered by method. Nvidia fell about 3%, Oracle nearly 6% and CoreWeave nearly 8% on 8 October, with AMD, Broadcom and Intel each down 4–5% *(Source : [CNBC — Nvidia, Oracle, CoreWeave and other AI stocks sink on OpenAI revenue report](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/08/open-ai-revenue-nvidia-oracle-coreweave.html))*, and Nvidia reportedly shed roughly $170bn in market value in a single session *(Source : [24/7 Wall St. — OpenAI's Revenue Is Reportedly $20 Billion Lower Than Thought. Nvidia Just Lost $170 Billion](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/09/openais-revenue-is-reportedly-20-billion-lower-than-thought-nvidia-just-lost-170-billion/))*. The logic is mechanical: OpenAI has committed to more than $1tn of compute and infrastructure spending across Oracle, Microsoft, Nvidia and others *(Source : [Tomasz Tunguz — OpenAI's $1 Trillion Infrastructure Spend](https://tomtunguz.com/openai-hardware-spending-2025-2035/))*. Those are bookings, not revenue, but they are underwritten by an expectation of OpenAI's future revenue; restate the numerator downward and the commitments look heavier.

## The postponed IPO and the $70bn target

OpenAI's public-market debut has slid to at least 2027, with Sam Altman framing the delay around AI-safety readiness rather than revenue *(Source : [Fortune — Sam Altman confirms OpenAI won't go public this year](https://fortune.com/2026/09/12/sam-altman-openai-ipo-delay-ill-advised-moment-safety-concerns/))*. The company is valued at $852bn post-money from its March round *(Source : [CNBC — OpenAI closes funding round at an $852 billion valuation](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/openai-funding-round-ipo.html))* and is reportedly in talks to raise at least $30bn at roughly a $1.4tn pre-money valuation *(Source : [Yahoo Finance — OpenAI Eyes $1.4 Trillion Valuation in New Funding](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-eyes-1-4-trillion-121524629.html))*. The valuation multiple depends entirely on which number you use: $1.4tn against $70bn is about 20 times, against $50bn about 28 times. OpenAI still expects to reach or exceed $70bn annualized by year-end, driven by enterprise, where it reported 107% run-rate growth in the third quarter. Meeting that target will be a claim about a rate, not a year.

## FAQ

**If OpenAI's annualized revenue is $50bn, why did headlines say $70bn a week earlier?**
Because the $68–70bn figure was an investor-side adjustment, not an OpenAI disclosure. It grossed up partner sales to match Anthropic's method and applied a growth rate on top. The FT reported the $50bn figure a week later as OpenAI's own basis.

**Did OpenAI lose $20bn of sales?**
No. Every major account — the FT, CNBC, CNN and TechCrunch — attributes the gap to accounting methodology, not a collapse in demand. The higher number and the lower number measure different things.

**Does the correction mean OpenAI is smaller than Anthropic?**
Not necessarily, and not on the numbers as reported. Anthropic's $65bn (end-July) includes gross cloud-partner sales; OpenAI's $50bn (end-September) does not. Ranking one against the other compares two definitions.

**What should actually be watched?**
Two things: the $70bn end-2026 target Bloomberg reported — a forecast of a year-end pace, not full-year revenue — and the eventual prospectus, where an auditor rather than an investor spreadsheet sets a single, defined basis.
