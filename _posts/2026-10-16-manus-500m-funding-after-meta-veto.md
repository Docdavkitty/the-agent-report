## TL;DR

- Butterfly Effect, the parent of the Manus agent, raised more than $500 million on October 8 — its first round since Beijing ordered Meta's $2 billion acquisition unwound.
- Boyu Capital and IDG Capital co-led, with existing shareholders Tencent, HSG and ZhenFund increasing their positions. The company disclosed no valuation; Bloomberg reported the round targets roughly $4 billion.
- That is double what Meta agreed to pay in December 2025. The gap created by the forced divestiture accrued entirely to the Chinese investors who bought the company back.
- Manus is also reported to be weighing a Hong Kong listing, which would give those investors an exit.

## The unwind, priced

The received wisdom about a blocked acquisition is that it damages the target. The last eight months at Manus suggest the opposite: the block was a transfer of value from a US buyer to Chinese institutions, executed at a fixed price.

Meta announced it would acquire Butterfly Effect for more than $2 billion in December 2025, and the deal closed on December 29. China's National Development and Reform Commission ordered the parties to unwind it in April 2026, citing foreign investment rules; the NDRC's security review office formally banned the transaction on April 27. Meta cut Manus from its internal data systems in June, with a required deletion of some user data attached. *(Source : [Quartz — Manus raises $500M after China blocked Meta acquisition](https://qz.com/manus-funding-round-meta-acquisition-china-100826))*

The original investors repurchased the company at the same $2 billion valuation, and Manus announced the restart of independent operations on September 1. *(Source : [36Kr — Secured 3.3 Billion Yuan Financing](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/4017148924350598))*

Eight days into October, Butterfly Effect announced in a WeChat post that it had raised more than $500 million. Boyu Capital and IDG Capital led the round, with Tencent, HSG (formerly Sequoia China) and ZhenFund adding to existing stakes. CNBC reported CATL among the participants. Manus did not disclose the use of funds or a post-money valuation. *(Source : [TechCrunch — China's Manus raises over $500M in first funding round since split with Meta](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/chinas-manus-raises-over-500m-in-first-funding-round-since-split-with-meta/))*

Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal had reported the round would land at roughly a $4 billion valuation — double the price Meta agreed to pay, and enough to place Manus ahead of every other AI agent company in China on paper. *(Source : [Mobile World Live — AI start-up Manus raises $500M after Meta split](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/big-tech/ai-start-up-manus-raises-500m-after-meta-split/))*

## The revenue underneath the round

Manus launched in March 2025 and went viral on the back of an agent demo. At the time Meta completed the acquisition, the company was pulling in annual recurring revenue of more than $100 million. By June, The Information reported that Manus had grown its annualized run rate to roughly $500 million, a fivefold increase in about six months. *(Source : [Quartz — Manus raises $500M after China blocked Meta acquisition](https://qz.com/manus-funding-round-meta-acquisition-china-100826))*

If both figures hold, the round is a growth story rather than a rescue. A $4 billion valuation against a $500 million run rate implies a multiple of eight, cheap next to the roughly 42 times run rate investors accepted for Nous Research this month, though the comparison is imperfect because Manus's figure is a run rate rather than audited revenue.

The structure of the company complicates any simple reading. Butterfly Effect was founded in China in 2022 and moved its corporate base to Singapore in 2025, before the Meta deal was announced. The NDRC's order made clear that offshore incorporation does not shield a transaction from Beijing when the underlying technology and talent originated in China — a structure critics had called "Singapore washing." *(Source : [Quartz — Manus raises $500M after China blocked Meta acquisition](https://qz.com/manus-funding-round-meta-acquisition-china-100826))*

The precedent reaches past Manus. Chinese regulators have tightened scrutiny of US investment in domestic startups working on advanced AI. *(Source : [AI Weekly — Manus Parent Butterfly Effect Raises $500M+ After Meta Exit](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/manus-parent-butterfly-effect-raises-500m-after-meta-exit))*

## What the money is buying

Manus shipped Manus 2.0 on September 28, ten days before the round was announced: a new Cascade agent harness, plus Cloud Computer, Automations, Manus Studio for professional creation, and Cue, a separate app for personal agents. *(Source : [Manus — Introducing Manus 2.0](https://manus.im/blog/introducing-manus-2-0))*

Cue is the product that has drawn the most attention, because it gives each individual agent its own email address, phone number, wallet and computer. The company's framing is blunt: "Manus 2.0 is not a version update." *(Source : [The Next Web — Manus 2.0 and Cue give AI agents their own email, phone and wallet](https://thenextweb.com/news/manus-2-0-cue-ai-agents-email-phone-wallet))*

That is also where the governance questions concentrate. Analysts quoted on the launch put the trade-off in plain terms: Gartner's Anushree Verma argues autonomy is running ahead of governance, while IDC's Sakshi Grover points to the portability of an agent's state as the thing buyers should interrogate. The advice to procurement teams is to ask what the agent can access, what state it retains, how quickly access can be revoked, and whether the workflows can be rebuilt elsewhere. *(Source : [Adyog Pulse — Manus 2.0 launches Cascade and Cue](https://pulse.adyog.com/insights/manus-2-agents-own-email-phone-wallet))*

An agent with a phone number, an email address and a funded wallet is a very different asset class from a chatbot. It can transact, be impersonated, and accumulate an identity that outlives the subscription. None of that is disqualifying, but it is the part of Manus's roadmap that makes the Hong Kong listing question harder than the revenue line suggests.

## The template this sets

Read the Manus sequence as a template and it is uncomfortably clean. A Chinese-founded AI startup relocates to Singapore, agrees to sell to a US buyer, has the deal blocked on national-interest grounds, is repurchased by its original investors at the signed price, and then raises a larger round from domestic capital at double that price, with a local public listing as the likely exit.

Every step benefited Chinese institutions and cost the blocked foreign buyer nothing except the opportunity. If that pattern holds for the next contested acquisition, more US investors will factor the unwind risk into their offer, which lowers the price Chinese founders can command from abroad and pushes them, structurally, toward domestic capital.

## FAQ

**How much did Manus actually raise?**
More than $500 million, announced on October 8 in a WeChat post by parent company Butterfly Effect. Boyu Capital and IDG Capital co-led, with Tencent, HSG and ZhenFund participating.

**What is the valuation?**
Undisclosed. Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal reported the round targets roughly $4 billion — double the $2 billion Meta agreed to pay in December 2025.

**Why did China block the Meta deal?**
The NDRC cited foreign investment rules and required the transaction to be unwound in April 2026. Broader concerns about losing AI talent and researchers to the West had been building since 2025.

**Does the Singapore relocation matter?**
It did not protect the deal. The NDRC's order established that moving the corporate base offshore does not remove a transaction from Beijing's authority when the technology and talent originated in China.
